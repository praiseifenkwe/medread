## 1. Structure, Synthesis & Genetics of Hemoglobin
Hemoglobin is a tetramer composed of 4 globin polypeptide chains, each enclosing an iron-protoporphyrin IX (heme) moiety.
* **Globin Gene Clusters:**
  * **$alpha$-Globin Gene Cluster:** Chromosome **16**. Contains $zeta$ (zeta) and two duplicated $alpha$-genes per haploid genome ($alpha_2$ and $alpha_1$). Normal genotype: $alphaalpha/alphaalpha$ (4 functional genes).
  * **$eta$-Globin Gene Cluster:** Chromosome **11**. Contains $epsilon$ (epsilon), $G_gamma$, $A_gamma$, $delta$ (delta), and $eta$ genes. Normal genotype: $eta/eta$ (2 functional genes).

## 2. Sickle Cell Disease (SCD)

### A. Molecular Defect & Pathophysiology
* **Mutation:** Single base-pair point mutation in the $eta$-globin gene on chromosome 11: **GAG $ightarrow$ GTG** at codon 6.
* **Amino Acid Substitution:** Non-polar **Valine** replaces negatively charged **Glutamic acid** ($eta^6	ext{Glu} ightarrow 	ext{Val}$).
* **Polymerization Cascade:** In the deoxygenated state, the hydrophobic valine at position 6 fits into a complementary hydrophobic pocket formed by phenylalanine-85 and leucine-88 on the $eta$-chain of an adjacent hemoglobin tetramer $ightarrow$ rapid aggregation into tactile **14-strand helical filaments (tactoids)** $ightarrow$ cellular distortion (sickling), increased intracellular viscosity, cellular dehydration via K-Cl cotransport and Gardos channel activation.

```
[Deoxygenation / Acidosis / Dehydration]
                    ↓
[HbS Polymerization into 14-strand fibers]
                    ↓
[RBC Membrane Damage & Sickle Deformation]
     ↙                                    ↘
[Extravascular & Intravascular Lysis]   [Microvascular Vaso-Occlusion]
- Chronic hemolytic anemia              - Tissue ischemia, infarction, pain crises
- Hyperbilirubinemia, pigment stones    - Organ damage (ACS, Stroke, Autosplenectomy)
- Endothelial dysfunction (low NO)      - Dactylitis in infants
```

### B. Sickle Cell Crises Matrix

| Crisis Type | Precipitating Factors | Clinical Features & Findings | Management Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Vaso-occlusive (Painful) Crisis** | Cold, dehydration, infection, stress | Severe bone/joint pain, dactylitis ("hand-foot syndrome") in infants, priapism | Aggressive IV hydration, analgesia (paracetamol $ightarrow$ weak opioids $ightarrow$ IV morphine), oxygen if hypoxic |
| **Acute Chest Syndrome (ACS)** | Fat embolism from infarcted marrow, infection (*Chlamydia*, *Mycoplasma*, *Pneumococcus*) | Fever, chest pain, tachypnea, hypoxemia, **new pulmonary infiltrate on CXR**. *Leading cause of SCD death.* | Broad-spectrum antibiotics, oxygen, cautious hydration, **early simple or exchange transfusion** |
| **Splenic Sequestration Crisis** | Viral infection in infants/toddlers prior to autosplenectomy | Sudden pooling of blood in spleen $ightarrow$ rapid fall in Hb, hypovolemic shock, massive tender splenomegaly | Urgent volume resuscitation and emergent red cell transfusion. Post-stabilization splenectomy considered |
| **Aplastic Crisis** | **Parvovirus B19** infection (destroys erythroid progenitors) | Sudden worsening of anemia **WITHOUT** reticulocytosis (**Reticulocyte count $< 0.1%$ / absent**), no splenomegaly | Transfusion support until bone marrow recovery (7–10 days). Self-limiting |
| **Hyperhemolytic Crisis** | G6PD deficiency co-inheritance, malaria, mycoplasma | Sudden drop in Hb, marked reticulocytosis, deepened jaundice, dark urine | Hydration, treat underlying trigger, red cell transfusion |

### C. Chronic Organ Complications
* **Spleen:** Repeated micro-infarctions in early childhood lead to progressive fibrosis and **Autosplenectomy** by age 5–7. Hallmarks: presence of **Howell-Jolly bodies** on peripheral blood film and high susceptibility to encapsulated bacteria (*Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae*).
* **Bones:** Avascular necrosis (AVN) of the femoral and humeral heads; *Salmonella* and *Staphylococcus aureus* osteomyelitis.
* **Kidneys:** Sickle nephropathy (renal papillary necrosis, isosthenuria/hyposthenuria, proteinuria).
* **Eyes:** Proliferative sickle retinopathy ("sea-fan" neovascularization).

---

### D. Laboratory Diagnosis & Electrophoresis
1. **Sickling Test:** Patient blood mixed with 2% sodium metabisulfite (reducing agent) under coverslip sealed with wax $ightarrow$ sickled forms appear within 1–2 hours. (Does NOT distinguish HbSS from HbAS trait).
2. **Hemoglobin Solubility Test:** Deoxygenated HbS precipitates in high-phosphate buffer containing dithionite, turning solution turbid. (False negatives in neonates due to high HbF).
3. **Cellulose Acetate Electrophoresis at Alkaline pH (8.4–8.6):**
   * Migration order from Cathode ($-$) to Anode ($+$):
     **$	ext{Origin} ightarrow 	ext{C} ightarrow 	ext{S} ightarrow 	ext{F} ightarrow 	ext{A}$** (*Mnemonic: "Crawl, Slow, Fast, Accelerate"*).
   * HbC migrates slowest (closest to cathode); HbA migrates fastest (closest to anode).
   * *Limitation:* HbS co-migrates with Hb D-Punjab and Hb G-Philadelphia. HbC co-migrates with HbA2 and HbE.
4. **Citrate Agar Electrophoresis at Acid pH (6.0–6.2):** Separates HbS from HbD, and HbC from HbE. Migration order: $	ext{F} ightarrow 	ext{A} ightarrow 	ext{S} ightarrow 	ext{C}$.
5. **High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC):** Gold standard for accurate quantification of HbA, HbA2, HbF, and variants.

## 3. The Thalassemias

### A. Alpha Thalassemia (Gene Deletions on Chromosome 16)
Normal genotype: $alphaalpha/alphaalpha$ (4 functional genes).
1. **Silent Carrier ($-alpha/alphaalpha$):** 3 genes intact. Asymptomatic; normal red cell indices.
2. **Alpha Thalassemia Trait:** 2 genes intact.
   * Asian deletion: $--/alphaalpha$ ($alpha^0$ in cis).
   * African deletion: $-alpha/-alpha$ ($alpha^+$ in trans).
   * Asymptomatic; mild microcytic hypochromic anemia (MCV $< 75$), normal Hb electrophoresis in adults.
3. **Hemoglobin H (HbH) Disease ($--/-alpha$):** 1 gene intact.
   * Excess $eta$-globin chains form unstable tetramers: **HbH ($eta_4$)**.
   * Moderate-to-severe hemolytic anemia, splenomegaly. Supravital stain shows **"golf-ball" inclusions** in erythrocytes.
4. **Hydrops Fetalis with Hb Barts ($--/--$):** 0 genes intact.
   * Excess $gamma$-chains form tetramers: **Hb Barts ($gamma_4$)**.
   * Extreme affinity for oxygen; releases no $O_2$ to tissues. Lethal *in utero* or shortly after birth with severe anasarca and hepatosplenomegaly.

---

### B. Beta Thalassemia (Point Mutations on Chromosome 11)
Normal genotype: $eta/eta$.
* **$eta^0$:** Complete absence of $eta$-globin synthesis.
* **$eta^+$:** Markedly reduced $eta$-globin synthesis.

1. **Beta Thalassemia Minor (Trait) ($eta/eta^0$ or $eta/eta^+$):**
   * Heterozygous. Mild microcytic hypochromic anemia ($	ext{Hb} 9–11 	ext{g/dL}$, $	ext{MCV} 60–75 	ext{fL}$, $	ext{RBC count} > 5.0 	imes 10^{12}/	ext{L}$, Mentzer Index $< 13$).
   * **Diagnostic Hallmark:** **HbA2 $> 3.5%$** (typically 4–6%) on HPLC or electrophoresis.
2. **Beta Thalassemia Major (Cooley's Anemia) ($eta^0/eta^0$ or $eta^+/eta^+$):**
   * Severe transfusion-dependent anemia manifesting at **4–6 months of age** (as HbF synthesis declines and fails to be replaced by HbA).
   * **Pathogenesis of Ineffective Erythropoiesis:** Unpaired, insoluble $alpha$-globin chains precipitate within erythroid precursors in the bone marrow $ightarrow$ membrane damage and intramedullary apoptosis of $> 80%$ of erythroblasts.
   * **Clinical Findings:** Severe pallor, "chipmunk facies" (malar prominence), skull X-ray showing **"hair-on-end" / "crew-cut" appearance** due to massive medullary expansion, hepatosplenomegaly, growth stunting.
   * **Complications:** Secondary hemosiderosis (endocrine failure, cirrhosis, cardiac arrhythmia) from chronic lifelong transfusions; requires iron chelation (Deferasirox, Deferoxamine).
