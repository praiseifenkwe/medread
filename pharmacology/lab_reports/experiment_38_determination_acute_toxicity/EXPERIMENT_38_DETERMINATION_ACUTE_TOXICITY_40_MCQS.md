# EXPERIMENT 38: DETERMINATION OF ACUTE TOXICITY (LD50)

1. The primary objective of Experiment 38 is to determine:

A. The chronic carcinogenic potential of a compound
B. The median lethal dose (LD_50) of a test substance in mice
C. The minimum inhibitory concentration against bacteria
D. The rate of renal tubular secretion

Answer: B

2. What is the standard pharmacological definition of LD_50?

B. The statistically derived single dose of a substance that kills 50% of an animal population under defined conditions
C. The dose of a drug that cures 50% of infected animals
D. The maximum tolerated dose with zero animal deaths

Answer: B

17. In the arithmetic method of Karber (Behrens-Karber), the formula for calculating LD_50 is:

A. LD_50 = LD_100 - [sum(z * d) / n]
B. LD_50 = LD_0 + [sum(z * d) * n]
C. LD_50 = (LD_100 + LD_0) / 2

Answer: A

18. In Karber's formula, what does "z" represent?

A. Total number of animals tested
B. The mean number of dead animals between two consecutive dose levels
C. The difference between body weights
D. The time of death in minutes

Answer: B

19. In Karber's formula, what does "d" represent?

A. Duration of the experiment
B. The difference between two consecutive dose levels
C. The number of survivors
D. The diameter of the syringe needle

Answer: B

20. In Karber's formula, what does "n" represent?

A. Number of animals per group
B. Total number of dead animals
C. Number of groups tested
D. Concentration of the drug solution

Answer: A

21. In Lorke's method (1983) for acute toxicity testing, how many phases are conducted?

A. One phase only
B. Two phases (Phase 1 screening followed by Phase 2 specific dosing)

22. In Lorke's Phase 1, what three initial doses are typically administered to groups of 3 mice each?

Answer: B

23. How is the final LD_50 calculated using Lorke's formula?

C. Difference between D_100 and D_0
D. Ratio of D_100 to D_0

Answer: B

24. What is the major ethical advantage of Lorke's method over classical probit analysis?

A. It uses far fewer experimental animals (typically 13 mice instead of 50–100 mice)
B. It does not require weighing the animals

Answer: A

25. What is the formula for the Therapeutic Index (TI) of a drug?

A. ED_50 / LD_50
B. LD_50 / ED_50
C. LD_50 * ED_50
D. (LD_50 - ED_50) / 100

Answer: B

26. A high Therapeutic Index (e.g., TI = 1,000) indicates that a drug:

A. Is extremely toxic and dangerous
B. Has a very wide margin of safety between effective and lethal doses
C. Is completely ineffective clinically
D. Cannot be absorbed orally

Answer: B

27. A low Therapeutic Index (e.g., TI < 2.0) indicates that a drug:

C. Has zero potential for toxicity

29. Why is the Certain Safety Factor (LD_1 / ED_99) clinically superior to the Therapeutic Index (LD_50 / ED_50)?
