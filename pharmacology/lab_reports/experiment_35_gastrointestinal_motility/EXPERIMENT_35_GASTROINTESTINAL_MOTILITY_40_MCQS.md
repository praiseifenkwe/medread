# EXPERIMENT 35: GASTROINTESTINAL MOTILITY TESTS
### 40 STANDARD IN-COURSE MCQs

1. The primary aim of Experiment 35 is to determine:

A. The gastric acid output in anesthetized rats
B. The effects of pharmacological agents on gastrointestinal motility and transit using the charcoal meal test
C. The degree of mucosal ulceration following ethanol challenge

Answer: B

19. In a mouse pre-treated with atropine, what effect is expected on the distance travelled by the charcoal front?

20. What is the molecular mechanism of atropine in reducing gastrointestinal transit?

B. Competitive blockade of muscarinic M_3 receptors on intestinal smooth muscle, inhibiting peristaltic contractions

D. Activation of beta-2 adrenergic receptors

21. What effect would a prokinetic drug like metoclopramide produce on intestinal transit?

A. Complete cessation of intestinal movement
B. Significant increase in charcoal transit distance and higher % motility
C. Severe intussusception
D. Discoloration of the intestinal wall to white

Answer: B

22. Metoclopramide accelerates upper gastrointestinal transit primarily by:

23. How do opioid analgesics (e.g., morphine, codeine, loperamide) affect intestinal motility?

24. Loperamide is widely used clinically as an antidiarrheal because:

25. Which division of the autonomic nervous system normally stimulates gastrointestinal motility and secretions?
