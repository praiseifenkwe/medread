## 🎯 High-Yield Conceptual Summary for In-Course Exams

1. **Pathophysiology of the Mouse Writhing Assay (Koster's Test / Witkin's Method):**
   * **Noxious Stimulus:** Intraperitoneal injection of **0.6% acetic acid** induces acute localized chemical peritonitis and visceral peritoneal inflammation.
   * **Pain Mediators Released:** Acetic acid triggers endogenous release of **prostaglandins ($	ext{PGE}_2, 	ext{PGF}_{2alpha}$)**, bradykinin, histamine, serotonin, substance P, and pro-inflammatory cytokines ($	ext{TNF}-alpha, 	ext{IL}-1eta, 	ext{IL}-8$) into the peritoneal fluid.
   * **Nociceptor Sensitization:** Prostaglandins sensitize primary visceral nociceptive afferent C-fibers, lowering their firing threshold.
2. **Behavioral Definition of a "Writhe":**
   * Characterized by a **wave of contraction of the abdominal musculature, elongation of the body, and backward extension of the hind limbs** (printed as *"extension of hind lings"*).
3. **Reference Standards (Central vs Peripheral):**
   * **Acetylsalicylic Acid (Aspirin - 100 mg/kg p.o.):** Peripherally acting reference standard. Irreversibly acetylates and inhibits cyclooxygenase enzymes (COX-1 and COX-2) $ightarrow$ stops local prostaglandin synthesis $ightarrow$ prevents peritoneal nociceptor sensitization.
   * **Morphine Sulphate (10 mg/kg s.c.):** Centrally acting reference standard. Agonist at $mu$-opioid receptors in the periaqueductal gray, spinal cord dorsal horn, and rostral ventromedial medulla $ightarrow$ inhibits substance P release and hyperpolarizes ascending pain projection neurons.
4. **Critical Exam Pitfall (Question 1 Mechanism):**
   * The mouse writhing test is a **non-specific algesic model**: it responds to **BOTH centrally acting analgesics (opioids)** and **peripherally acting analgesics (NSAIDs)**!
   * *How to differentiate in an exam:* To confirm whether a plant extract acts centrally or peripherally, it must be evaluated alongside a **thermal nociceptive model** (such as the Hot Plate or Tail Flick test). Centrally acting opioids inhibit both thermal and chemical pain; peripherally acting NSAIDs inhibit chemical writhing but are **ineffective against acute thermal pain**!
5. **Pre-treatment Interval (30 Minutes):**
   * Acetic acid is injected **30 minutes after drug administration** to allow complete gastrointestinal absorption and systemic distribution of oral agents (distilled water, aspirin, unknown extract) and subcutaneous morphine.

### Question 17: Pharmacological Rationale for Morphine Route (s.c.)
**Why is Morphine administered subcutaneously (s.c.) in Group III while Acetylsalicylic Acid and the Unknowns are given orally (p.o.)?**
