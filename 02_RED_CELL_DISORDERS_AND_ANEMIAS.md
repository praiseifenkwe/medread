## 1. Classification of Anemia
Anemia is defined as a reduction in circulating hemoglobin concentration, hematocrit (PCV), or red cell count below age- and sex-adjusted normal reference limits.
* **WHO Cut-offs:** Men $< 13.0 	ext{g/dL}$ (PCV $< 39%$), Non-pregnant Women $< 12.0 	ext{g/dL}$ (PCV $< 36%$), Pregnant Women $< 11.0 	ext{g/dL}$ (PCV $< 33%$).

## 2. Iron Metabolism & Microcytic Anemias

### A. Physiology of Iron Balance
* **Total Body Iron:** 3–4 g (65% in Hemoglobin, 25% stored as Ferritin/Hemosiderin, 10% in Myoglobin/Cytochromes, $< 1%$ bound to transferrin).
* **Daily Turnover:** Absorbed 1–2 mg/day from duodenum; daily obligatory loss 1–2 mg/day (desquamated gut/skin enterocytes; menstruation increases loss by ~0.5–1 mg/day). No physiological excretory mechanism for iron.
* **Dietary Forms:**
  * **Heme Iron (ferrous, $	ext{Fe}^{2+}$):** In animal products (meat, poultry). Highly bioavailable (20–30% absorbed intact).
  * **Non-Heme Iron (ferric, $	ext{Fe}^{3+}$):** In plant foods. Poorly bioavailable (1–10%); requires reduction to $	ext{Fe}^{2+}$ by apical duodenal ferric reductase (**DCYTB**) before entering via Divalent Metal Transporter 1 (**DMT1**).
* **Export and Transport:** Iron exits basolateral membrane through **Ferroportin (Fpn)**, oxidized by **Hephaestin** ($	ext{Fe}^{2+} ightarrow 	ext{Fe}^{3+}$), and binds plasma **Transferrin** (each molecule binds 2 ferric ions).
* **The Master Regulator: HEPCIDIN**
  * Synthesized by hepatocytes in response to iron loading and inflammation (IL-6).
  * **Mechanism:** Binds ferroportin $ightarrow$ internalizes and degrades it $ightarrow$ blocks duodenal iron absorption and traps iron inside reticuloendothelial macrophages.
  * Low iron / hypoxia $ightarrow$ suppresses hepcidin $ightarrow$ maximal iron mobilization.

## 3. Megaloblastic Anemia: Folate vs Cobalamin (Vitamin B12)

### A. Metabolic Pathways & Neuropathy Mechanism
* **Biochemical Crossroads:**
  1. **Homocysteine $ightarrow$ Methionine:** Requires both **Vitamin B12** (methylcobalamin) and **5-Methyl-THF**. Defect in either traps folate as 5-methyl-THF ("Methyl-Folate Trap"), impairing thymidylate synthesis and DNA replication.
  2. **Methylmalonyl-CoA $ightarrow$ Succinyl-CoA:** Requires **Vitamin B12** (adenosylcobalamin). Defect leads to accumulation of **Methylmalonic Acid (MMA)**, causing defective myelin synthesis and **Subacute Combined Degeneration of the Spinal Cord (SCD)**.
* **Key Distinction:** Folate deficiency elevates **Homocysteine ONLY**. B12 deficiency elevates **BOTH Homocysteine and Methylmalonic Acid (MMA)**.
* **⚠️ Fatal Exam Pitfall:** Treating a B12-deficient patient with high-dose folate corrects the megaloblastic anemia but **accelerates irreversible subacute combined degeneration of the spinal cord**!

### B. Clinical Comparison

| Parameter | Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin) | Folate (Vitamin B9) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dietary Source** | Animal products only (meat, liver, eggs, dairy) | Green leafy vegetables, citrus fruits, liver |
| **Body Stores** | Large (3–5 mg in liver); takes **3–5 years** to deplete | Small (5–10 mg); takes **3–4 months** to deplete |
| **Absorption Site** | Terminal ileum (requires gastric **Intrinsic Factor**) | Upper jejunum / duodenum (as monoglutamates) |
| **Neurological Deficits** | **Yes:** Paresthesias, loss of vibration/position, spastic ataxia (SCD) | **No** (purely hematological) |
| **Etiologies** | Pernicious anemia (autoimmune anti-IF/parietal cells), gastrectomy, Crohn's ileitis, *Diphyllobothrium latum* | Dietary malnutrition, pregnancy, hemolytic anemia (increased turnover), Methotrexate, alcohol |
| **Peripheral Blood Film** | Oval macrocytes, **Hypersegmented Neutrophils** ($ge 5$ lobes in $> 5%$ of PMNs) | Identical to B12 |
| **Bone Marrow** | **Nuclear-cytoplasmic dyssynchrony** (large immature chromatin-open nucleus with mature hemoglobinized cytoplasm) | Identical to B12 |

## 4. Hemolytic Anemias

### A. Intravascular vs Extravascular Hemolysis (Core Departmental Table)

| Laboratory Parameter | Extravascular Hemolysis | Intravascular Hemolysis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Site of Lysis** | Spleen and Liver macrophages (cords of Billroth) | Within blood vessels (complement, shear, toxins) |
| **Serum Unconjugated Bilirubin** | Markedly Elevated | Mildly to Moderately Elevated |
| **Serum Haptoglobin** | Decreased or Normal | **Absent / Markedly Decreased** |
| **Plasma Free Hemoglobin** | Normal | **Markedly Elevated (Hemoglobinemia)** |
| **Urine Hemoglobin** | **Absent** | **Present (Hemoglobinuria - dark coca-cola urine)** |
| **Urine Hemosiderin (Prussian Blue)**| Negative | **Positive (shed renal tubular cells 4–7 days post-event)**|
| **Serum LDH** | Moderately Elevated | Markedly Elevated |
| **Morphologic Clue** | **Spherocytes** (Hereditary Spherocytosis, Warm AIHA) | **Schistocytes / Helmet cells** (MAHA, TTP, DIC, prosthetic valve) |

---

### B. Specific Hemolytic Entities

#### 1. Hereditary Spherocytosis (HS)
* **Inheritance & Defect:** Autosomal dominant (75%). Deficiency of RBC membrane skeleton proteins (**Ankyrin** [most common], **Band 3**, **$eta$-Spectrin**).
* **Pathogenesis:** Loss of membrane surface area without volume loss $ightarrow$ sphere-shaped cell $ightarrow$ reduced deformability $ightarrow$ trapped and destroyed in splenic sinusoids.
* **Diagnostics:** Elevated **MCHC ($> 36 	ext{g/dL}$)**, negative DAT, **Osmotic Fragility Test** (increased lysis in hypotonic saline), **Eosin-5-Maleimide (EMA) Binding test** by flow cytometry (gold standard).
* **Treatment:** Splenectomy (curative for hemolysis; defer until age $> 5$ to minimize OPSI risk).

#### 2. Glucose-6-Phosphate Dehydrogenase (G6PD) Deficiency
* **Inheritance & Defect:** X-linked recessive. Defective G6PD impairs NADPH generation via hexose monophosphate shunt $ightarrow$ inability to maintain reduced Glutathione $ightarrow$ vulnerability to oxidative stress.
* **Triggers:** Infections (most common), Fava beans (favism), Drugs (Primaquine, Dapsone, Cotrimoxazole, Nitrofurantoin).
* **Pathology:** Oxidized hemoglobin precipitates into **Heinz Bodies** (seen on supravital stain). Splenic macrophages pluck out inclusions, creating **Bite Cells (Degmacytes)** and blister cells.
* **⚠️ Diagnostic Warning:** G6PD enzyme assay must **NOT** be performed during an acute hemolytic attack (reticulocytes are young and rich in G6PD, yielding a false-normal result). Retest 2–3 months later.

#### 3. Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemias (AIHA)
* **Warm AIHA (80%):** **IgG** autoantibodies reacting optimally at **37°C**. Extravascular hemolysis in spleen. Spherocytes present. DAT positive for IgG $pm$ C3d. Responsive to corticosteroids, rituximab, splenectomy.
* **Cold AIHA (20%):** **IgM** autoantibodies reacting optimally at **$< 32°C$**. Fixes complement; causes intravascular and extravascular (liver) lysis. Associated with *Mycoplasma pneumoniae*, EBV, and lymphomas. Peripheral smear shows **red cell agglutination**. DAT positive for C3d ONLY. Unresponsive to steroids or splenectomy; manage by keeping patient warm and rituximab.

## 5. Bone Marrow Failure & Aplastic Anemia
* **Pathophysiology:** T-cell ($CD8+$ cytotoxic) mediated autoimmune destruction of hematopoietic stem cells; oligoclonal expansion producing IFN-$gamma$ and TNF-$alpha$.
* **Etiologies:** Idiopathic ($> 70%$), Drugs (Chloramphenicol, gold, carbamazepine), Viral hepatitis (seronegative), Benzene, Fanconi anemia (chromosomal breakage).
* **Camitta Criteria for Severe Aplastic Anemia (SAA):**
  * Bone Marrow Biopsy Cellularity $< 25%$ (or $< 50%$ if $< 30%$ hematopoietic cells).
  * PLUS at least 2 of 3 peripheral blood criteria:
    1. Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) $< 0.5 	imes 10^9/	ext{L}$ (Very Severe if $< 0.2 	imes 10^9/	ext{L}$).
    2. Platelet Count $< 20 	imes 10^9/	ext{L}$.
    3. Reticulocyte Count $< 20 	imes 10^9/	ext{L}$ (or $< 1%$).
* **Bone Marrow Smear:** "Dry tap" on aspiration; trephine biopsy shows **profound hypocellularity replaced by adipose tissue**, devoid of blast cells or fibrosis.
