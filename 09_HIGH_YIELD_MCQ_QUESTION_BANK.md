## SECTION 1: BLOOD COMPONENT THERAPY & STORAGE (Questions 1 – 20)

### Q1: Which blood component is rich in clotting factors and is used to treat bleeding disorders?
* **Options:** a) Platelets | b) Fresh Frozen Plasma | c) Red Blood Cells | d) Granulocyte concentrates
* **Correct Answer:** **b) Fresh Frozen Plasma**
* **Rationale:** Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP) is prepared from whole blood and frozen within 8 hours. It contains normal physiological concentrations of all soluble coagulation factors (Factors I through XIII), protein C, protein S, and antithrombin.
* **Exam Pearl:** Platelets provide primary hemostatic plug elements, while FFP supplies secondary coagulation factors.

---

### Q2: What is the standard shelf life of packed red blood cells when stored properly at 2°C to 6°C?
* **Options:** a) 14 days | b) 50 days | c) 42 days | d) 60 days
* **Correct Answer:** **c) 42 days**
* **Rationale:** When collected in modern additive solutions like SAGM (Saline-Adenine-Glucose-Mannitol), PRBCs have a maximum shelf life of **42 days**. (In standard CPDA-1, shelf life is 35 days).

---

### Q3: What is the primary function of cryoprecipitate in blood component therapy?
* **Options:** a) To provide oxygen-carrying capacity | b) To enhance clotting capabilities | c) To restore plasma volume | d) To boost immune response
* **Correct Answer:** **b) To enhance clotting capabilities**
* **Rationale:** Cryoprecipitate is the cold-insoluble gelatinous precipitate recovered from thawed FFP. It is heavily concentrated in **Fibrinogen ($ge 150 	ext{mg/unit}$)**, **Factor VIII**, **von Willebrand Factor**, and **Factor XIII**.

---

### Q4: Which blood component therapy is best used to replace clotting factors and fibrinogen in patients with documented fibrinogen deficiencies?
* **Options:** a) Platelet Transfusion | b) Whole Blood Transfusion | c) Fresh Frozen Plasma Transfusion | d) Cryoprecipitate Transfusion
* **Correct Answer:** **d) Cryoprecipitate Transfusion**
* **Rationale:** While FFP contains fibrinogen, cryoprecipitate provides a vastly higher concentration in a small volume (~15–20 mL per unit), preventing fluid overload when raising fibrinogen levels above $100–150 	ext{mg/dL}$.

---

### Q5: What is the most common adverse reaction to a blood transfusion?
* **Options:** a) Allergic reaction | b) Hemolytic reaction | c) Febrile non-hemolytic reaction | d) Hypotension
* **Correct Answer:** **c) Febrile non-hemolytic reaction (FNHTR)**
* **Rationale:** FNHTR accounts for $> 50%$ of all reported transfusion reactions (affecting 1–2% of transfusions). It is triggered by recipient antibodies reacting against donor white cell antigens or pyrogenic cytokines (IL-1, IL-6, TNF-$alpha$) that leak from leukocytes during storage.

---

### Q6: In blood component therapy, what is the purpose of leukoreduction?
* **Options:** a) To remove platelets | b) To remove red blood cells | c) To remove leukocytes (white blood cells) | d) To remove plasma
* **Correct Answer:** **c) To remove leukocytes (white blood cells) from the blood**
* **Rationale:** Pre-storage leukoreduction filters out $> 99.9%$ of donor white cells ($< 1 	imes 10^6$ residual leukocytes per unit).
* **Clinical Benefits of Leukoreduction:**
  1. Prevents Febrile Non-Hemolytic Transfusion Reactions (FNHTR).
  2. Prevents HLA alloimmunization (platelet refractoriness).
  3. Prevents transmission of leukocyte-borne viruses (e.g., Cytomegalovirus / CMV).

---

### Q7: What is the typical storage temperature for Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP)?
* **Options:** a) Room temperature | b) 2°C to 6°C | c) -20°C (-4°F) or below | d) 37°C
* **Correct Answer:** **c) -20°C (-4°F) or below**
* **Rationale:** FFP must be maintained at **$-18°C$ to $-30°C$ (or colder)** to preserve the activity of labile coagulation factors (Factor V and Factor VIII). At this temperature, it has an approved shelf life of **12 months**.

---

### Q8: Which blood component therapy is used to treat thrombocytopenia and platelet function disorders?
* **Options:** a) Platelet Transfusion | b) Whole Blood Transfusion | c) Fresh Frozen Plasma | d) Cryoprecipitate
* **Correct Answer:** **a) Platelet Transfusion**
* **Rationale:** Platelet concentrates (either apheresis or pooled random donor units) are indicated for prophylactic coverage in severe thrombocytopenia ($< 10 	imes 10^9/	ext{L}$) or therapeutic control of microvascular mucosal bleeding in thrombocytopenic/thrombocytopathic patients.

---

### Q9: Which blood type is considered the universal donor for packed red blood cells?
* **Options:** a) A+ | b) O- | c) AB+ | d) B-
* **Correct Answer:** **b) O- (O Rhesus Negative)**
* **Rationale:** Group O- red blood cells lack A, B, and D antigens on their membrane. Therefore, they will not be attacked by anti-A, anti-B, or anti-D antibodies in the recipient's circulation.
* **Exam Trap:** **AB+ is the universal recipient for RBCs**, but **AB is the universal donor for PLASMA** (plasma contains no anti-A or anti-B antibodies).

---

### Q10: What is the primary purpose of irradiating blood products before transfusion?
* **Options:** a) To eliminate bacteria | b) To prevent viral transmission | c) To prevent Transfusion-Associated Graft-versus-Host Disease (TA-GVHD) | d) To enhance shelf-life
* **Correct Answer:** **c) To prevent Transfusion-Associated Graft-versus-Host Disease (TA-GVHD)**
* **Rationale:** Gamma irradiation (25–30 Gy) damages donor lymphocyte DNA, preventing viable donor T-cells from proliferating and attacking host tissues in immunocompromised recipients or shared-HLA directed donations.

---

### Q11: What is the recommended storage temperature for Platelet Concentrates?
* **Options:** a) 2°C to 6°C | b) 20°C to 24°C | c) -18°C | d) 37°C
* **Correct Answer:** **b) 20°C to 24°C (Room temperature with continuous agitation)**
* **Rationale:** Cold storage causes irreversible platelet activation and rapid clearance from circulation upon infusion. Therefore, platelets must be stored at room temperature ($20–24°C$) on an agitator.
* **Consequence:** Short shelf life (maximum **5 days**) and highest risk of bacterial contamination among all blood products.

---

### Q12: What is the typical shelf life of Platelets stored at 20°C to 24°C?
* **Options:** a) 24 hours | b) 5 days | c) 21 days | d) 35 days
* **Correct Answer:** **b) 5 days**

---

### Q13: Cryoprecipitate contains high concentrations of all of the following EXCEPT:
* **Options:** a) Fibrinogen | b) Factor VIII | c) von Willebrand Factor | d) Factor IX
* **Correct Answer:** **d) Factor IX**
* **Rationale:** Cryoprecipitate is rich in **Fibrinogen, Factor VIII, vWF, and Factor XIII**. Factor IX remains in the supernatant plasma and is **NOT** concentrated in cryoprecipitate (Hemophilia B must be treated with Factor IX concentrates or FFP, NOT cryoprecipitate).

---

### Q14: A unit of packed red blood cells transfused into an average non-bleeding 70-kg adult is expected to raise the hemoglobin level by approximately:
* **Options:** a) 0.5 g/dL | b) 1.0 g/dL | c) 2.5 g/dL | d) 3.0 g/dL
* **Correct Answer:** **b) 1.0 g/dL (or PCV by ~3%)**

---

### Q15: In an emergency scenario requiring uncrossmatched red cells before typing is complete, which blood group must be selected?
* **Options:** a) AB Rh-positive | b) O Rh-negative | c) O Rh-positive | d) A Rh-negative
* **Correct Answer:** **b) O Rh-negative (specifically uncrossmatched O-negative PRBCs)**

---

### Q16: What is the primary risk associated with the administration of Fresh Frozen Plasma?
* **Options:** a) Hypocalcemia | b) Fluid overload and allergic reactions | c) Graft versus host disease | d) Thrombocytopenia
* **Correct Answer:** **b) Fluid overload (TACO) and allergic reactions**
* **Rationale:** FFP is administered in substantial volumes ($10–15 	ext{mL/kg}$); infusing 3–4 units ($800–1000 	ext{mL}$) rapidly can easily precipitate cardiogenic pulmonary edema (TACO) in patients with compromised cardiovascular or renal reserves.

---

### Q17: Which virus has the highest risk of transmission per unit of transfused blood in unscreened populations?
* **Options:** a) HIV | b) Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) | c) Hepatitis C Virus (HCV) | d) HTLV-1
* **Correct Answer:** **b) Hepatitis B Virus (HBV)**
* **Rationale:** Due to high viral loads, pre-seroconversion "window periods", and high baseline endemicity in tropical regions like West Africa.

---

### Q18: What is the definitive test performed to detect maternal antibodies in maternal serum that could cause HDFN?
* **Options:** a) Direct Antiglobulin Test (DAT) | b) Indirect Antiglobulin Test (IAT) | c) Acid elution test | d) Osmotic fragility test
* **Correct Answer:** **b) Indirect Antiglobulin Test (IAT)**
* **Rationale:** IAT detects free circulating antibodies in the mother's serum *in vitro*. The DAT detects antibodies already bound to fetal/neonatal red blood cells *in vivo*.

---

### Q19: Which blood component has the highest risk of transmitting bacterial sepsis?
* **Options:** a) Packed Red Blood Cells | b) Fresh Frozen Plasma | c) Platelet Concentrates | d) Cryoprecipitate
* **Correct Answer:** **c) Platelet Concentrates**
* **Rationale:** Platelets are stored at room temperature ($20–24°C$), which provides an ideal growth environment for contaminating skin flora (*Staphylococcus epidermidis*, *Staphylococcus aureus*) and gram-negative bacilli.

---

### Q20: What is the primary purpose of performing a Major Crossmatch?
* **Options:** a) To check donor ABO type | b) To detect recipient antibodies reactive against donor red blood cells | c) To assess donor platelet function | d) To quantitate hemoglobin
* **Correct Answer:** **b) To detect recipient antibodies reactive against donor red blood cells**
* **Rationale:** Major Crossmatch $=$ Patient's Serum $+$ Donor's Red Blood Cells. If recipient serum contains antibodies against donor antigens, agglutination or hemolysis occurs, alerting the laboratory that transfusion would trigger an acute hemolytic reaction.

## SECTION 2: ADVERSE REACTIONS & CLINICAL VIGNETTES (Questions 21 – 77)

### Q21: What is the immediate, life-threatening transfusion reaction caused by ABO incompatibility?
* **Options:** a) Allergic reaction | b) Acute Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction (AHTR) | c) FNHTR | d) Anaphylactic reaction
* **Correct Answer:** **b) Acute Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction (AHTR)**

---

### Q22: What is the most common cause of Acute Hemolytic Transfusion Reactions?
* **Options:** a) Laboratory reagent failure | b) Human clerical / patient identification errors | c) Bacterial contamination | d) Storage lesion
* **Correct Answer:** **b) Human clerical / patient identification errors (bedside misidentification)**

---

### Q23: A patient receiving PRBCs develops fever ($39°	ext{C}$), rigors, severe flank pain, dark reddish urine, and hypotension within 15 minutes of transfusion onset. What is the diagnosis?
* **Options:** a) TRALI | b) Acute Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction (AHTR) | c) TACO | d) FNHTR
* **Correct Answer:** **b) Acute Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction (AHTR)**
* **High-Yield Triad:** **Fever/Rigors + Flank/Back Pain + Hemoglobinuria (Red/Dark urine)**.

---

### Q24: What is the immediate first action required when an acute transfusion reaction is suspected?
* **Options:** a) Administer IV diphenhydramine | b) Slow down the infusion rate | c) STOP the transfusion immediately | d) Call the consultant
* **Correct Answer:** **c) STOP the transfusion immediately and disconnect at the IV hub**

---

### Q25: An Anaphylactic transfusion reaction is a life-threatening emergency characteristically seen in patients with:
* **Options:** a) Sickle cell disease | b) Selective IgA deficiency with anti-IgA antibodies | c) G6PD deficiency | d) Hemophilia A
* **Correct Answer:** **b) Selective IgA deficiency with anti-IgA antibodies**
* **Rationale:** IgA-deficient individuals recognize normal donor IgA as a foreign antigen, triggering massive IgE-mediated mast cell degranulation.
* **Hallmark:** Occurs within minutes, features bronchospasm, stridor, and shock **WITHOUT FEVER**.

---

### Q26: A 25-year-old trauma patient receives multiple units of blood and develops sudden-onset, severe respiratory distress. Chest X-ray reveals bilateral fluffy infiltrates. Blood pressure is 85/50 mmHg, JVP is normal, and BNP is normal. What is the diagnosis?
* **Options:** a) TACO | b) TRALI | c) Anaphylaxis | d) Myocardial infarction
* **Correct Answer:** **b) TRALI (Transfusion-Related Acute Lung Injury)**
* **Key Distinguishers from TACO:**
  * TRALI is **Non-Cardiogenic Pulmonary Edema** $ightarrow$ Normal/low BP, normal JVP, normal BNP.
  * TACO is **Cardiogenic Pulmonary Edema** $ightarrow$ High BP, elevated JVP, elevated BNP, responds to diuretics.

---

### Q27: What is the primary pathogenic mechanism in TRALI?
* **Options:** a) Volume overload | b) Donor anti-HLA or anti-HNA antibodies activating recipient pulmonary neutrophils | c) Recipient anti-IgA antibodies | d) Histamine release
* **Correct Answer:** **b) Donor anti-HLA or anti-HNA antibodies activating recipient pulmonary neutrophils**
* **Epidemiology:** Multiparous female donors have high rates of HLA alloimmunization; modern blood banks use male-only or nulliparous female plasma to reduce TRALI.

---

### Q28: An elderly patient with chronic kidney disease receives 2 units of PRBCs rapidly. Two hours later, he develops orthopnea, cough with frothy sputum, BP 180/100 mmHg, elevated JVP, and bilateral basilar crackles. What is the most effective immediate therapy?
* **Options:** a) IV Epinephrine | b) IV Furosemide (diuretic) and oxygen | c) IV Hydrocortisone | d) Broad-spectrum antibiotics
* **Correct Answer:** **b) IV Furosemide (diuretic) and oxygen**
* **Diagnosis:** **Transfusion-Associated Circulatory Overload (TACO)**.

---

### Q29: A 42-year-old male with severe aplastic anemia receives 2 units of non-irradiated PRBCs from his sister (a haploidentical match). Ten days later, he presents with fever, erythroderma with bullae, watery diarrhea, jaundice, and pancytopenia. What is the diagnosis?
* **Options:** a) Delayed hemolytic transfusion reaction | b) Transfusion-Associated Graft-versus-Host Disease (TA-GVHD) | c) TRALI | d) Acute promyelocytic leukemia
* **Correct Answer:** **b) Transfusion-Associated Graft-versus-Host Disease (TA-GVHD)**
* **Mechanism:** The donor's viable T-cells recognize host HLA as foreign, whereas the immunocompromised recipient fails to reject donor cells. The donor T-cells mount an attack against host tissues, especially bone marrow.
* **Prognosis:** Mortality $> 90%$.
* **Prevention:** **Gamma-irradiation of blood products**.

---

### Q30: A multiparous woman develops severe purpura and acute profound thrombocytopenia (platelet count $< 10 	imes 10^9/	ext{L}$) exactly 7 days after a blood transfusion. What is the diagnosis?
* **Options:** a) Immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) | b) Post-Transfusion Purpura (PTP) | c) TTP | d) DIC
* **Correct Answer:** **b) Post-Transfusion Purpura (PTP)**
* **Mechanism:** Anamnestic production of alloantibodies against human platelet antigens (usually **Anti-HPA-1a**), which paradoxically destroys both donor and autologous recipient platelets. Treated with IVIG or plasmapheresis.

---

### Q31: If an air embolism is suspected during rapid pressure infusion of blood, what position should the patient be immediately placed in?
* **Options:** a) Trendelenburg position on the left side (Durant maneuver) | b) Reverse Trendelenburg on the right side | c) Prone position | d) Upright sitting position
* **Correct Answer:** **a) Trendelenburg position on the left side (Durant maneuver)**
* **Anatomical Rationale:** Placing the patient in the left lateral decubitus position with head down traps the air bubble in the apex of the right ventricle, preventing it from obstructing the right ventricular outflow tract and pulmonary artery.

---

### Q32: A patient receiving a platelet transfusion develops sudden-onset fever of 40°C, rigors, tachycardia, and profound hypotension within 30 minutes. What is the diagnosis and immediate management?
* **Options:** a) AHTR $ightarrow$ IV saline | b) Transfusion-transmitted bacterial sepsis $ightarrow$ Stop transfusion, blood cultures from patient and blood bag, immediate broad-spectrum IV antibiotics | c) TRALI $ightarrow$ Ventilator | d) Allergic reaction $ightarrow$ Antihistamines
* **Correct Answer:** **b) Transfusion-transmitted bacterial sepsis**
* **Pearl:** Platelets stored at room temperature have the highest risk of gram-negative sepsis (*Serratia*, *Enterobacter*, *Pseudomonas*) and skin flora (*Staphylococcus*).

---

### Q33: Delayed Hemolytic Transfusion Reactions (DHTR) typically occur 5 to 10 days post-transfusion and are most classically associated with anamnestic antibodies against which blood group system?
* **Options:** a) ABO system | b) Kidd (Jk) antigen system | c) Lewis system | d) Secretor system
* **Correct Answer:** **b) Kidd (Jk) antigen system**
* **Rationale:** Anti-Jka and Anti-Jkb antibodies frequently drop to undetectable levels in pre-transfusion antibody screening. Re-exposure triggers an intense anamnestic IgG response 5–10 days later, presenting with mild jaundice, falling hemoglobin, and a positive DAT.
