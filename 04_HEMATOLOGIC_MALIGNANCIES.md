## 1. Classification & Diagnostic Foundations
Hematological malignancies are clonal proliferations of transformed hematopoietic stem or progenitor cells.
* **Acute Leukemias:** Proliferation of immature, non-functional cells (**blasts**) that arrest differentiation. Diagnostic threshold: **$ge 20%$ blasts** in bone marrow or peripheral blood (WHO classification; previously $ge 30%$ in FAB). Rapidly progressive; fatal in weeks to months if untreated.
* **Chronic Leukemias:** Proliferation of mature, differentiated elements. Indolent clinical course; survival often measured in years.

## 2. Acute Leukemias: AML vs ALL Comparison

| Feature | Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML) | Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia (ALL) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Peak Incidence** | Adults (median age 65; incidence rises with age) | Children (peak age **2–5 years**; 80% of childhood leukemias) |
| **Blast Morphology** | Large blasts, moderate cytoplasm, fine granular chromatin, 2–5 distinct nucleoli. **Auer rods present** | Small-to-medium blasts, scanty cytoplasm, dense/coarse chromatin, indistinct nucleoli. **NO Auer rods** |
| **Cytochemistry** | **Myeloperoxidase (MPO) Positive**<br>**Sudan Black B (SBB) Positive**<br>Non-specific Esterase (NSE) positive in monoblastic (M4/M5) | **MPO Negative**<br>**Sudan Black Negative**<br>**PAS (Periodic Acid-Schiff) Block Positive** |
| **Immunophenotype** | **CD13+, CD33+, CD117+, MPO+**, $pm$ HLA-DR | **B-ALL:** CD19+, CD20+, CD22+, **CD10 (CALLA)+**, **TdT+**<br>**T-ALL:** CD2+, CD3+, CD5+, CD7+, **TdT+** |
| **Extramedullary Disease** | Gingival hypertrophy (M4/M5), chloromas (granulocytic sarcomas), leukemia cutis | **High affinity for Sanctuary Sites:** CNS and Testes; mediastinal/thymic mass in T-ALL |

### Special Focus: Acute Promyelocytic Leukemia (APML / AML-M3)
* **Cytogenetics:** Reciprocal translocation **$t(15;17)(q24;q21)$**, fusing the Promyelocytic Leukemia (*PML*) gene on chromosome 15 with the Retinoic Acid Receptor Alpha (*RARA*) gene on chromosome 17.
* **Morphology:** Promyelocytes packed with azurophilic granules and bundles of Auer rods resembling a bundle of sticks (**"Faggot Cells"**).
* **Life-Threatening Emergency:** High risk of fulminant **Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)** due to release of procoagulants (tissue factor) and annexin II from neoplastic promyelocyte granules.
* **Targeted Differentiation Therapy:** **All-Trans Retinoic Acid (ATRA)** + **Arsenic Trioxide (ATO)**. ATRA dissociates the PML-RARA corepressor complex, forcing promyelocytes to differentiate into mature granulocytes. Cures $> 90%$ without cytotoxic chemotherapy.

## 3. Chronic Leukemias: CML vs CLL

### A. Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML)
* **Pathophysiology:** Myeloproliferative neoplasm characterized by the **Philadelphia Chromosome (Ph)**, representing reciprocal translocation **$t(9;22)(q34;q11.2)$**.
* **Molecular Product:** Translocates the *ABL1* proto-oncogene from chromosome 9 to the *BCR* gene on chromosome 22, generating the **BCR-ABL1 fusion gene**. This encodes a **p210 chimeric protein with constitutively active tyrosine kinase activity**, driving autonomous granulocytic proliferation.
* **Clinical Phases:**
  1. **Chronic Phase (3–5 years):** Marked leukocytosis ($50–300 	imes 10^9/	ext{L}$), massive splenomegaly, weight loss. Blood film shows full spectrum of myeloid differentiation ("myelocyte bulge").
  2. **Accelerated Phase:** Rising blast count (10–19%), worsening splenomegaly, basophilia $ge 20%$, persistent thrombocytopenia.
  3. **Blast Crisis:** **$ge 20%$ blasts** in marrow/blood. 70% transform to AML, 30% to ALL.
* **Crucial Diagnostic Test: Leukocyte Alkaline Phosphatase (LAP) Score**
  * **CML:** LAP score is **characteristically LOW or ZERO** (malignant neutrophils lack LAP enzyme).
  * **Leukemoid Reaction (reactive infection/stress):** LAP score is **MARKIDLY HIGH**.
* **Targeted Therapy:** **Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors (TKIs):** **Imatinib (Gleevec)**, followed by 2nd-generation agents (Nilotinib, Dasatinib) for resistance/intolerance. Completely suppresses BCR-ABL1 and restores normal life expectancy.

---

### B. Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL)
* **Pathophysiology:** Clonal accumulation of morphologically mature, functionally incompetent $CD5+ B$-lymphocytes in blood, bone marrow, and lymphoid tissue. Most common leukemia in older adults ($> 60$ years).
* **Blood Film:** Dense monomorphic lymphocytosis of small cells with clumped "soccer-ball" chromatin and scanty cytoplasm. Presence of fragile disrupted cells known as **Smudge / Basket Cells**.
* **Immunophenotype:** **CD19+, CD20+ (dim), CD23+, and aberrantly CD5+** (a T-cell marker).
* **Staging Systems (Rai & Binet):**
  * **Rai System:** Stage 0 (Lymphocytosis only) $ightarrow$ Stage I (Lymphadenopathy) $ightarrow$ Stage II (Splenomegaly/Hepatomegaly) $ightarrow$ Stage III (Anemia, $	ext{Hb} < 11 	ext{g/dL}$) $ightarrow$ Stage IV (Thrombocytopenia, Platelets $< 100 	imes 10^9/	ext{L}$).
  * **Binet System:** Stage A ($< 3$ lymph node areas) $ightarrow$ Stage B ($ge 3$ areas) $ightarrow$ Stage C (Anemia $	ext{Hb} < 10 	ext{g/dL}$ or Platelets $< 100 	imes 10^9/	ext{L}$).
* **Complications:**
  1. Hypogammaglobulinemia $ightarrow$ recurrent bacterial infections (leading cause of death).
  2. Autoimmune Cytopenias: Warm AIHA ($10–15%$) and immune thrombocytopenia.
  3. **Richter Transformation (5–10%):** Sudden transformation into aggressive Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma (DLBCL), presenting with rapid localized node enlargement and systemic B-symptoms.

## 4. The Lymphomas: Hodgkin vs Non-Hodgkin

### A. Comparative Diagnostic Matrix

| Parameter | Hodgkin Lymphoma (HL) | Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma (NHL) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cell of Origin** | Germinal center B-cell transformed into **Reed-Sternberg (RS) cell** | Clonal B-cell (85%), T-cell (15%), or NK-cell |
| **Nodal Involvement** | Contiguous spread along anatomically adjacent lymph node chains | Non-contiguous, erratic spread; "skip" areas |
| **Extranodal Presentation** | Rare ($< 5%$) | **Common (30–40%):** GI tract, bone marrow, Waldeyer's ring, skin, CNS |
| **Mediastinal Mass** | Frequent (especially Nodular Sclerosis) | Less common (except Lymphoblastic or Primary Mediastinal B-cell) |
| **B-Symptoms** | Common (40%); carries major prognostic significance (Ann Arbor "B") | Less frequent in low-grade; common in high-grade |
| **Curability** | **High ($> 80–90%$)** with ABVD chemotherapy | Variable; indolent types are incurable; aggressive types curable in 50–60% with R-CHOP |

---

### B. Reed-Sternberg (RS) Cell & HL Subtypes
* **RS Cell Morphology:** Giant multinucleated or bilobed cell with prominent, eosinophilic inclusion-like nucleoli surrounded by a clear halo, creating an **"owl-eyed" appearance**.
* **Immunophenotype of Classic HL:** **CD15+, CD30+, CD20- (loss of B-cell phenotype), PAX5+ (weak)**.
* **WHO Subtypes of Classic HL:**
  1. **Nodular Sclerosis (65–70%):** Most common. Young adults/females. Collagen bands dividing lymph node into nodules; contains **Lacunar RS cells**. Frequent mediastinal involvement.
  2. **Mixed Cellularity (20–25%):** Older adults, male predominance. Associated with **EBV ($> 70%$)**. Abundant diagnostic RS cells in a polymorphic background of eosinophils, plasma cells, and histiocytes.
  3. **Lymphocyte-Rich (5%):** Best prognosis among classic HL; few RS cells, abundant mature mantle-zone lymphocytes.
  4. **Lymphocyte-Depleted ($< 2%$):** Worst prognosis. High RS-to-lymphocyte ratio; common in elderly and HIV-positive patients.
* **Non-Classic Variant:** **Nodular Lymphocyte Predominant HL (NLPHL):** Displays **"Popcorn Cells"** (Lymphocytic & Histiocytic [L&H] cells); immunophenotype is **CD20+, CD45+, CD15-, CD30-**.

---

### C. Major Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma Subtypes
* **Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma (DLBCL):** Most common NHL overall (30–40%). Rapidly growing symptomatic mass. Aggressive but curable with **R-CHOP** (Rituximab + Cyclophosphamide, Doxorubicin, Vincristine, Prednisolone).
* **Follicular Lymphoma:** Most common indolent NHL. Translocation **$t(14;18)(q32;q21)$**, fusing *IGH* with *BCL2*, causing constitutive overexpression of the anti-apoptotic protein **BCL2**.
* **Burkitt Lymphoma:** Highly aggressive; doubling time $< 24$ hours; Ki-67 proliferation index $approx 100%$.
  * **Cytogenetics:** Translocation **$t(8;14)(q24;q32)$**, fusing the *MYC* oncogene with *IGH*.
  * **Histology:** **"Starry-Sky" Pattern** (sheets of uniform basophilic B-cells representing the "dark sky", punctuated by tingible-body macrophages ingesting apoptotic debris representing "stars").
  * **Endemic Variant:** Young children in equatorial Africa; strongly linked to **EBV** and malaria; classic presentation is a **rapidly expanding jaw / facial bone mass**.

## 5. Multiple Myeloma & Plasma Cell Disorders
* **Definition:** Malignant proliferation of clonal post-germinal center plasma cells in the bone marrow, accompanied by monoclonal immunoglobulin production.
* **The Diagnostic CRAB Criteria (End-Organ Damage):**
  * **C - HyperCalcemia:** Serum calcium $> 11.0 	ext{mg/dL}$ (due to osteolytic destruction driven by RANKL and MIP-1$alpha$).
  * **R - Renal Insufficiency:** Serum creatinine $> 2.0 	ext{mg/dL}$ (primarily "Myeloma Cast Nephropathy" caused by toxic light chains precipitating with Tamm-Horsfall protein).
  * **A - Anemia:** Normocytic normochromic ($	ext{Hb} < 10.0 	ext{g/dL}$ or $> 2 	ext{g/dL}$ below normal).
  * **B - Bone Lesions:** One or more osteolytic "punched-out" lesions on skeletal survey, CT, or MRI (vertebrae, skull, ribs, pelvis). *Pathological fractures and bone pain.*
* **Laboratory Hallmarks:**
  * **Serum Protein Electrophoresis (SPEP):** Dense, narrow-based **M-Spike** (monoclonal paraprotein, 60% IgG, 20% IgA).
  * **Urine:** **Bence Jones Proteinuria** (free monoclonal $kappa$ or $lambda$ light chains; missed on standard protein dipstick, detected by sulfosalicylic acid test or immunofixation).
  * **Peripheral Blood Smear:** **Rouleaux Formation** ("stacks of coins") caused by high paraprotein charge neutralizing negative red cell surface repulsive forces.
  * **Bone Marrow Aspirate:** $ge 10%$ clonal plasma cells. Cells exhibit eccentric round nuclei, clumped **"clock-face" / "cartwheel" chromatin**, abundant deep basophilic cytoplasm, and a prominent **perinuclear clear halo (Golgi apparatus)**.

## 6. Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (MPN)
Clonal stem cell disorders marked by proliferation of one or more non-lymphoid myeloid lineages:
1. **Polycythemia Vera (PV):** Predominant erythrocytosis. **$> 95%$ harbor the $JAK2 V617F$ mutation**. Clinical features: Plethoric facies, aquagenic pruritus (itching after warm baths due to basophil degranulation), splenomegaly, low serum erythropoietin.
2. **Essential Thrombocythemia (ET):** Sustained thrombocytosis ($> 450 	imes 10^9/	ext{L}$). Mutations: $JAK2$ (55%), $CALR$ (25%), $MPL$ (5%). Hemorrhage and thrombosis.
3. **Primary Myelofibrosis (PMF):** Clonal megakaryocytes secrete PDGF and TGF-$eta$, causing progressive reactive marrow fibrosis (reticulin/collagen).
   * **Hallmarks:** Massive splenomegaly (extramedullary hematopoiesis), "dry tap" on marrow aspirate, and a **Leukoerythroblastic Blood Picture** with classic **Teardrop Poikilocytes (Dacryocytes)**.

## 7. Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation (HSCT)
* **Autologous HSCT:** Patient's own stem cells harvested, stored, and re-infused following high-dose myeloablative conditioning. Primary role: Multiple Myeloma and relapsed lymphomas (provides rescue from marrow toxicity).
* **Allogeneic HSCT:** Stem cells from HLA-matched sibling, matched unrelated donor (MUD), or haploidentical relative. Provides graft-versus-leukemia (GVL) effect; curative for AML, ALL, Severe Aplastic Anemia.
* **Graft-versus-Host Disease (GVHD):**
  * **Acute GVHD ($< 100$ days):** Donor T-lymphocytes attack host tissues. Clinical triad: **Skin** (erythematous maculopapular rash starting on palms/soles), **Liver** (cholestatic jaundice, elevated conjugated bilirubin/alkaline phosphatase), **GI Tract** (profuse secretory green watery diarrhea, cramping).
  * **Chronic GVHD ($> 100$ days):** Scleroderma-like skin changes, sicca syndrome (dry eyes/mouth), bronchiolitis obliterans.
