## 1. Red Cell Antigen Systems

### A. The ABO Blood Group System
The ABO antigens are terminal carbohydrate epitopes attached to core glycoprotein and glycolipid chains on RBC membranes and vascular endothelial cells:
* **Precursor H Substance:** Synthesized by the $H$ gene (fucosyltransferase), adding **L-fucose** to the precursor carbohydrate chain. Genotype: $H/H$ or $H/h$.
* **Group A:** The $A$ allele encodes $alpha$-1,3-N-acetylgalactosaminyltransferase, adding **N-acetylgalactosamine (GalNAc)** to the H antigen.
* **Group B:** The $B$ allele encodes $alpha$-1,3-D-galactosyltransferase, adding **D-galactose** to the H antigen.
* **Group AB:** Both enzymes are expressed; both GalNAc and D-galactose are added.
* **Group O:** The $O$ allele contains a single nucleotide deletion ($261	ext{delG}$) producing a non-functional truncated transfer酶; the H substance remains unmodified.

* **The Bombay Phenotype ($hh$):**
  * Rare homozygous recessive genotype ($hh$) lacking the fucosyltransferase enzyme.
  * Cannot synthesize H substance; therefore cannot add A or B sugars regardless of ABO genotype.
  * Cells type as Group O on forward grouping, but serum contains **potent, wide-thermal-amplitude Anti-H, Anti-A, and Anti-B**.
  * Can **ONLY** be transfused with blood from another Bombay ($hh$) individual!

### B. The Rhesus (Rh) System
* **Antigens:** Non-glycosylated transmembrane polypeptides encoded by two closely linked genes on chromosome 1: **$RHD$** (encodes D antigen) and **$RHCE$** (encodes C/c and E/e antigens).
* **Clinical Significance:** **RhD is the most potent and immunogenic red cell antigen** after ABO. Approximately 85% of Caucasians and $> 95%$ of Black Africans are RhD-positive.
* **Key Distinction from ABO:** Anti-D antibodies are **NOT naturally occurring**. They are only formed following exposure to foreign RhD-positive red cells via blood transfusion or feto-maternal hemorrhage during pregnancy/delivery.
* **Class of Anti-D:** **IgG** (subclasses IgG1 and IgG3). Readily crosses the placenta and mediates extravascular hemolysis in the fetal spleen.

## 2. Hemolytic Disease of the Fetus and Newborn (HDFN)

### A. RhD Incompatibility vs ABO Incompatibility

| Parameter | RhD Incompatibility HDFN | ABO Incompatibility HDFN |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Parental Setup** | Mother: **RhD-Negative**; Baby: **RhD-Positive** | Mother: **Group O**; Baby: **Group A or B** |
| **Occurrence in 1st Pregnancy** | **Very Rare ($< 1%$)** (requires prior sensitizing event) | **Common (40–50%)** (maternal anti-A,B IgG already exists) |
| **Severity of Hemolysis** | **Severe to life-threatening; Hydrops Fetalis** | **Mild to moderate; rarely severe** |
| **Cord Blood Anemia** | **Severe (Hb often $< 8 	ext{g/dL}$)** | Mild or absent (Hb usually normal/mildly low) |
| **Direct Antiglobulin Test (DAT)** | **Strongly Positive ($3+$ to $4+$)** | **Weakly Positive ($1+$) or Negative** |
| **Peripheral Blood Film** | Marked erythroblastosis (nucleated RBCs), polychromasia; **NO spherocytes** | **Prominent Spherocytes**, polychromasia |
| **Why is ABO HDFN Milder?** | D antigen is dense and exclusive to RBCs | 1. A/B antigens are weakly expressed on neonatal RBCs<br>2. A/B antigens are widely expressed on tissues/endothelium, soaking up antibody |

### B. Pathophysiology of Severe Rh HDFN & Hydrops Fetalis
1. Rh-negative mother carries Rh-positive fetus $ightarrow$ feto-maternal hemorrhage during delivery of 1st child ($> 0.1 	ext{mL}$ fetal blood) $ightarrow$ maternal sensitization and production of anti-D memory B-cells.
2. In subsequent Rh-positive pregnancy $ightarrow$ maternal anti-D IgG crosses placenta $ightarrow$ coats fetal RBCs $ightarrow$ massive splenic extravascular hemolysis.
3. **The Hydrops Cascade:**
   * Severe fetal anemia ($	ext{Hb} < 6 	ext{g/dL}$) $ightarrow$ high-output cardiac failure.
   * Massive compensatory **Extramedullary Hematopoiesis** in the fetal liver and spleen $ightarrow$ profound hepatosplenomegaly.
   * Liver architectural disruption by proliferating erythroblasts $ightarrow$ hepatocellular dysfunction, portal hypertension, and **hypoalbuminemia**.
   * Hypoalbuminemia + high hydrostatic pressure $ightarrow$ systemic anasarca: **ascites, pleural effusion, pericardial effusion, scalp edema (Hydrops Fetalis)**.
4. **Postnatal Bilirubin Danger (Kernicterus):**
   * *In utero*, unconjugated bilirubin is cleared across the placenta by maternal liver.
   * After birth, neonatal liver (immature UDP-glucuronosyltransferase) cannot handle the bilirubin load. Unconjugated bilirubin rises rapidly ($> 20 	ext{mg/dL}$ / $340 mu	ext{mol/L}$), crosses the blood-brain barrier, and deposits selectively in the **basal ganglia, subthalamic nuclei, and hippocampus (Kernicterus)**, causing choreoathetoid cerebral palsy, sensorineural deafness, and cognitive impairment.

### C. Prevention & Management Protocol
* **Anti-D Immunoglobulin (RhIG / RhoGAM):**
  * Routine dose: **$300 mu	ext{g}$ ($1500 	ext{IU}$)** administered IM to all unsensitized Rh-negative pregnant women at **28 weeks gestation** and within **72 hours of delivery** of an Rh-positive infant.
  * Also given after any potential sensitizing event: miscarriage, termination, amniocentesis, ectopic pregnancy, abdominal trauma.
  * **Mechanism:** Binds and clears fetal Rh-positive red cells from maternal circulation before maternal immune recognition occurs.
  * **Kleihauer-Betke Test:** Acid-elution smear on maternal blood. Adult HbA elutes out (ghost cells), while fetal HbF resists acid and stains bright pink. Quantifies feto-maternal hemorrhage to calculate extra anti-D vials needed ($1 	ext{vial of } 300 mu	ext{g}$ covers $30 	ext{mL}$ fetal whole blood or $15 	ext{mL}$ packed RBCs).
* **Treatment of Neonatal Jaundice:**
  * **Phototherapy (Blue-green light, $460–490 	ext{nm}$):** Converts toxic hydrophobic 4Z,15Z-bilirubin into water-soluble structural isomer **Lumirubin (4Z,15E)**, which is excreted in bile and urine **without conjugation**.
  * **Double-Volume Exchange Transfusion:** Indicated if cord bilirubin $> 85 mu	ext{mol/L}$, rate of rise $> 8.5 mu	ext{mol/L/h}$, or bilirubin reaches exchange threshold. Removes antibody-coated RBCs, unloads bilirubin, and corrects anemia.

### B. High-Yield Reaction Profiles

#### 1. Acute Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction (AHTR)
* **Cause:** Clerical/administrative identification error leading to ABO-incompatible red cell transfusion.
* **Mechanism:** Preformed recipient IgM anti-A or anti-B binds donor RBCs $ightarrow$ fulminant complement activation ($C5b-9$) $ightarrow$ massive intravascular hemolysis and shock.
* **Presentation:** Triad of **Fever/Rigors**, **Flank / Back Pain**, and **Red/Dark Urine (Hemoglobinuria)**; hypotension, tachycardia, oozing from venipuncture sites (DIC).
* **Immediate Management:**
  1. **STOP TRANSFUSION IMMEDIATELY**. Disconnect set at hub.
  2. Maintain venous access with 0.9% normal saline.
  3. Re-check patient wristband and blood unit label.
  4. Send blood unit, administration set, and fresh post-transfusion recipient blood + urine samples to blood bank.
  5. Aggressive IV fluid resuscitation (aim for urine output $> 100–200 	ext{mL/h}$) to prevent acute tubular necrosis from free hemoglobin precipitation.

#### 2. Febrile Non-Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction (FNHTR)
* **Most common adverse reaction** (1–2% of transfusions).
* **Cause:** Recipient antibodies against donor white cells or accumulation of pyrogenic cytokines (IL-1, IL-6, TNF-$alpha$) released by leukocytes during storage.
* **Features:** Temperature rise of $ge 1°C$ above baseline during or within 2 hours of transfusion, chills, shivering. **NO hemolysis, NO hypotension**.
* **Prevention:** Pre-storage **Leukoreduction** of blood products.

#### 3. Anaphylactic Transfusion Reaction
* **Cause:** Occurs in **IgA-deficient individuals** who have developed anti-IgA antibodies; triggered by tiny volumes ($< 10 	ext{mL}$) of blood containing normal donor IgA.
* **Features:** Sudden onset of stridor, bronchospasm, facial/laryngeal edema, severe hypotension, cardiovascular collapse **WITHOUT fever**.
* **Management:** Immediate **Intramuscular Epinephrine (1:1000, 0.5 mL)**, oxygen, IV fluids. Future transfusions require **thoroughly washed red cells** or blood from IgA-deficient donors.

#### 4. TRALI vs TACO (Crucial Differential Diagnosis Table)

| Clinical Feature | TRALI (Transfusion-Related Acute Lung Injury) | TACO (Transfusion-Associated Circulatory Overload) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pathophysiology** | Donor anti-HLA or anti-HNA antibodies activate recipient neutrophils in pulmonary capillaries $ightarrow$ microvascular permeability | Excessive fluid volume infusion overwhelming cardiac capacity |
| **Type of Pulmonary Edema**| **Non-Cardiogenic Pulmonary Edema** | **Cardiogenic Pulmonary Edema** |
| **Blood Pressure** | **Normal or Hypotensive** | **Significantly Hypertensive** |
| **Jugular Venous Pressure**| Normal | **Elevated (JVD present)** |
| **Brain Natriuretic Peptide (BNP)**| **Normal** | **Markedly Elevated** |
| **Chest X-Ray** | Bilateral diffuse fluffy infiltrates | Cardiomegaly, bilateral infiltrates, pleural effusions |
| **Response to Diuretics (Furosemide)**| **No response (may worsen hypotension)** | **Rapid and dramatic clinical improvement** |
| **Prevention** | Exclude multiparous female donors from plasma/platelet donation | Slow transfusion rate ($1 	ext{mL/kg/h}$); prophylactic furosemide |

#### 5. Transfusion-Associated Graft-versus-Host Disease (TA-GVHD)
* **Mechanism:** Viable donor T-lymphocytes transfused to an immunocompromised recipient (or HLA-haploidentical related donor) engraft and mount an immune attack against host tissues.
* **Presentation:** Occurs 1–4 weeks post-transfusion with fever, erythroderma, profuse diarrhea, liver dysfunction, and **profound pancytopenia secondary to bone marrow aplasia**.
* **Prognosis:** **Fatal in $> 90%$ of cases**. Resistant to high-dose immunosuppression.
* **Absolute Prevention:** **Gamma-irradiation (25–30 Gy)** of cellular blood components prior to transfusion (inactivates donor T-cell proliferation while preserving red cell/platelet function).
